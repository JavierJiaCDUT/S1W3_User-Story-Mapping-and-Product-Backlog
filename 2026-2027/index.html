01 / Our fictional caseCHC5056 / 2026–2027

Carry the Evidence Forward

A student is organising a group meeting and requests a room.

E1 / INTERVIEW

“I emailed reception to check my request.” One simulated account, not a frequency claim.

E2 / OBSERVATION

“Request sent” is followed by an enquiry. Observed sequence does not establish the reason.

E3 / OBSERVATION

Two requests can compete for the same room and time.

B1 / SIMULATION RULE

Only one confirmed booking per room and time.

N1 / CANDIDATE NEED

Know whether the room is secured before telling the group.

H1 remains a hypothesisClearer status will reduce follow-up checking. We still need to investigate.

These identifiers continue the Week 2 teaching case. E1 and E2 inform a candidate need but do not prove its prevalence. Clearer status is a proposed response; reduced checking is an untested benefit hypothesis. E3 and rule B1 are established here and reused in the ordering and slice decisions.

02 / Product VisionCHC5056 / 2026–2027

A Vision Connects Need to Benefit

For students arranging group meetings, RoomLink is a room-request service that helps them know whether a room is secured before coordinating with their group.

Compared with a request-sent message alone, it aims to provide an explicit confirmed or unsuccessful outcome.

WHO + NEED

Meeting organisers

They need a clear booking outcome.

INTENDED BENEFIT

Coordinate with confidence

The expected benefit still needs checking.

PROPOSED DIFFERENCE

Outcome, not just receipt

Compared with this case’s current workflow.

A Product Vision gives a product a direction by linking its users, need and intended benefit. Differentiation needs a meaningful comparison. Here the comparison is with the fictional request-sent workflow, not a claim about every competing product.

02 / Direction and targetCHC5056 / 2026–2027

Vision and Product Goal

PRODUCT VISION

Why this product?

Help meeting organisers coordinate with confidence by making booking outcomes clear.

PRODUCT GOAL

What future state?

Eligible students can complete a room request and see an unambiguous outcome in one service.

Related, with different jobs

The vision frames the benefit. The Product Goal gives the team a future product state to plan towards.

Teaching examples. A vision statement is not a required Scrum artefact or a prescribed template.

In Scrum, the Product Goal is the commitment associated with the Product Backlog. Product Vision is a useful broader direction; Scrum does not mandate this particular vision format. A feature checklist does not automatically express either.

03 / Read the hierarchyCHC5056 / 2026–2027

Three Levels of a User Journey

ACTIVITY

Secure a booking

A broad purpose in the journey.

STEP

Check outcome

Something the user does within that activity.

STORY

See the outcome

As a meeting organiser, I want to see whether my booking is confirmed so I can tell my group.

Read levels in context. Granularity varies; these labels are our mapping convention.

An activity groups related user steps. A step describes something the user does. A story describes a more specific user-valued capability or variation supporting the step. Short map cards stand for conversations rather than complete specifications.

Keep the connectionUse stable IDs or links. Do not create two unrelated copies of the same work.

A backlog may include other product work, not only user stories. Story mapping is a complementary technique.

A map makes the journey and missing connections visible. A Product Backlog makes the current order of work explicit. Linking the same item between views avoids losing its context; the map does not replace the backlog.

04 / PBI-03 · Outcome storyCHC5056 / 2026–2027

Make One Item Understandable

US1

As a meeting organiser, I want to see the outcome of my request so I can tell my group whether we have a room.

SOURCE / NEED

E1 + E2 → N1

Reports of checking inform a need for an explicit outcome.

DEPENDENCY

PBI-02: Safe booking result

Show confirmed only after an eligible booking is recorded.

ACCEPTANCE EXAMPLES

Success and conflict

Success: room + time + confirmed.
Conflict: no extra booking + unsuccessful.

OPEN QUESTION

H1: less follow-up checking?

Passing behaviour checks does not establish this user benefit.

Useful teaching fields, not a mandatory Scrum item template. Simulation rule B1 still applies.

Traceability exposes the reasoning behind an item. Evidence informs N1; design and rule B1 shape acceptance examples. The result must be safe before a confirmation is shown. H1 requires separate investigation with users.

04 / Evidence, value and dependencyCHC5056 / 2026–2027

Order the Work for a Reason

Assume a room/slot feed is available. This is one provisional order for discussion.

01PBI-02 · Safe booking resultIntegrity risk: prevent conflicting confirmations.
02PBI-03 · See the outcomeValue for N1; depends on PBI-02.
03PBI-01 · Browse slotsCompletes the first path; depends on the assumed slot feed.
04PBI-04 · Theme customisationNo evidence of relevance to N1 yet.
Change the assumptionIf the slot feed is uncertain, investigate it early. Explain why the order changes.

The map reads across as the user’s narrative; the backlog orders work by risk, dependency and evidence. PBI-02 belongs to the first slice; attending to it early reflects risk, not layer-first delivery.

This order is conditional, not a delivery schedule. The complete first slice still needs browsing. In this example the core booking risk motivates early attention; if access to slots is uncertain, investigate that dependency earlier. Evidence can change the order.

Which Item Deserves Attention First?

CASE CONDITIONS

Two requests can compete

E3 reports conflicts. Rule B1 permits only one confirmed booking per room/time.

CHOICE

Outcome integrity or appearance?

A: handle a taken slot honestly.

B: add custom colour themes.

Ordering is a judgement with reasons. A small or visually impressive item is not automatically the highest-value one. Dependencies, risk and evidence can justify attention to less visible capabilities.

05 / RefinementCHC5056 / 2026–2027

New Evidence Changes the Work

NEW SIMULATED FEEDBACK / E4

One participant reads “request sent” as “booking confirmed”.

01 / CLARIFY

Separate receipt from outcome

Ask what the participant understood; check the workflow.

02 / BREAK DOWN

Make the item more precise

Clarify confirmed and unsuccessful cases, messages and checks.

03 / REORDER

Revisit near-term work

Address ambiguous outcomes, then reassess lower-value options.

A continuing loopLearn → clarify → adjust the backlog → learn again.

Refinement develops shared understanding as information changes. It can clarify, split and reorder items, not merely add text. E4 is one simulated observation and does not establish prevalence or validate a proposed solution.

05 / A collaborative decisionCHC5056 / 2026–2027

Refine What Matters Next

NEAR-TERM ITEM

“Show booking status”

High value, ambiguous outcome wording, unresolved conflict behaviour.

DISTANT IDEA

“Offer custom themes”

Low-order idea with no supporting evidence in this case.

Product Owner: accountable for backlog management. Collaboration does not mean one person writes every item alone.

Focus refinement effort on work whose uncertainty matters soon. User perspectives, feasibility and dependencies inform the conversation. The Product Owner remains accountable for backlog management even when others help do the work.
